### 3.5 Station selection (orienteering) and solver benchmark

**Tour (E5).** Given stations with a value, the astronaut should visit the subset that collects the most value within the energy budget, the EVA duration, optional time windows and the walkback rule. Stations are snapped to the graph like the habitat. One Dijkstra per station gives the directed minimum-energy cost between every pair, with the walking time measured along that same leg. The leg cost ratio i→j / j→i has percentiles 5/50/95 of 0.625/1.0/1.599, so the order of a tour matters. Service costs service time × 205 W. Up to 20 nodes the problem is solved exactly by a bitmask dynamic program [@HeldKarp1962], with Pareto labels when time also binds. Larger instances (the manual stations plus E8's sand-rock contacts, 27 nodes) are solved by OR-Tools CP-SAT [@ORTools], counted only when it proves the optimum. After solving, the tour is checked against the walkback rule and re-planned if it fails. Station time windows can come from the light: each station is served only while it is sunlit and out of the terrain's shadow, and the departure time is swept (Section 3.9). When no tour exists, the planner says why: if no station fits even on its own, no tour can, and the answer names the limiting resource, the shortfall and what would let the closest station fit; a solver timeout is reported as such, not as infeasibility. Station values come from a rubric reviewed against the literature (ridge 5, hollow 4, contact 4, Curiosity site 3). Its sensitivity is tested with seven fixed rubrics, 500 random order-preserving ones, and service times × 1.5.

**Benchmark (E6).** The exact solvers are compared with four metaheuristics: iterated local search [@Vansteenwegen2009], simulated annealing [@Kirkpatrick1983], ant colony system [@Dorigo1997] and a genetic algorithm. The instances are 60 random ones (N 10–18, with and without time windows), the 49 classic Tsiligirides instances [@Tsiligirides1984] and the four real E5 instances. Each method runs with one parameter setting; the stochastic ones run with three seeds.
